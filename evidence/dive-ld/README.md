## Purpose

Convert the Dive-LD case into an inspectable maritime-autonomy evidence package while separating public facts from proposed readiness instrumentation.

## Public Case

Public reporting on 8–9 September 2026 identified a U.S. autonomous underwater vehicle recovered by Iran near the Strait of Hormuz as an Anduril Dive-LD. Reuters reported that the Pentagon acknowledged the loss and said the older vehicle had malfunctioned more than a day earlier and did not contain sensitive data or classified sonar/radar equipment. DefenseScoop quoted CENTCOM saying the vehicle was effectively dead in the water for more than a day. Anduril characterized Dive-LD as an attritable autonomous system intended for dangerous environments where loss is an expected possibility.

Naval News initially assessed imagery as closely matching Dive-LD and later updated its report after additional imagery. The exact circumstances of recovery and internal U.S. contingency actions are not established by this pack.

## Failure Mode

A degraded, unresponsive, or lost autonomous platform stops being only a vehicle-state problem and becomes a recovery, exposure, mission-continuity, handoff, and decision-authority problem.

## Judgment Under Test

**At what point does an anomalous autonomous-platform state require a different decision regime?**
